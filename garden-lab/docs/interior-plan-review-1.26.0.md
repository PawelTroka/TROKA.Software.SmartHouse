# Sternicza 11 interior drawing review

The historical sheets support a four-level reconstruction, with two habitable storeys between an earth-floored cellar and a trussed attic. The marked scheme opens the ground floor, adds a shared balcony and divides the former upstairs front room into two small guest rooms, a corridor and a wardrobe. The garage remains a separate conversion study. Its default lanes cross the three-flue stack and the edge of the cellar stair.

The ground-floor design places a 3.18 × 1.04 m dining table in the left part of the newly connected rear rooms and a U-shaped sofa on the right. The TV moves onto the solid part of the right wall between the side window and transverse wall. Three cabinet runs wrap the front-left kitchen, with a low run under the street window. The former east kitchen and bath have one entrance beside the stair and form an approximately 11.22 m² bathroom.

The HST drawing allowance is 9.97 × 2.35 m between 0.40 m end returns. Both outdoor platforms measure 10.77 × 4.00 m, or 43.08 m² each. The upper balcony has three independent threshold-level access openings. The third rear opening appears only on the markup. The historical rear wall and portions of the transverse ground-floor wall would change. Locate joists, posts, ring beams, flues and foundations before designing a support system. Resolve the cellar areaway beneath the terrace, balcony support and thresholds, runoff and waterproofing together.

## Two-car cellar conversion

In the interior studio, Original drawings shows the source cellar. Your renovation shows the separate under-house two-car study using the current ramp width, proposed headroom, slab and lowering settings. Viewing this comparison leaves the outdoor ramp switch under the garden controls. The separate outbuilding elevations do not establish a garage inside this cellar.

At the default 5.80 m width there are two 2.90 m lanes. The shared Cyberster envelopes measure 4.535 m long, 1.913 m at the body and 2.110 m across mirrors. This gives 0.395 m between the outer mirrors and lane edges, 0.79 m between adjacent mirrors and about 7.00 m of flat length in the paper-plan reconstruction. Car doors, opening arcs and turning manoeuvres need separate checks.

For the default ramp, the mapped façade midpoint is around site x = 9.644 m and the portal centre is x = 11.752 m. Centring the 10.77 m drawing and reversing its x-axis puts that portal at drawing x = 3.277 m. Its clear width covers drawing x = 0.377–6.177 m. The three-flue core occupies x = 4.18–4.57 m within the second lane. The central cellar stair occupies x = 5.88–7.00 m and overlaps the lane edge by approximately 0.30 m. With 0.30 m at the rear wall the parked car extends to z = 5.235 m and can also intersect the core. The model retains both obstacles. Their overlap makes the unmodified structure incompatible with the straight approach even when vehicle widths and ramp slope separately pass.

A 2.30 m clear garage under a 0.30 m floor zone places the floor at −2.60 m beneath ground-floor datum, 0.60 m below the drawn cellar floor and 0.10 m below the foundation mark on this section. The lower floor would change the relationship between the existing stair, areaway and foundations. Establish footing geometry, stair reconstruction, flue continuity, excavation support, water table, drainage and the portal lintel before deciding whether a conversion can proceed.

## Reproducible checks

The interior model test compares garage registration with the actual mapped ramp frame across four widths, including a narrow case that fails the two-car allowance. It checks printed room schedules, opening widths and heights, fitted-object footprints against their room and the flues, and upper-floor door connectivity from the landing with the stair void and cores blocked. Further checks cover balcony access, both 4 m decks, the unchanged source cellar and the section-derived attic envelope. Reachability checks model point movement through open doors. Detailed accessibility, furniture delivery, door clashes and code compliance remain design questions.
