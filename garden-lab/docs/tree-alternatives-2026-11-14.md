# Tree alternatives at the same three positions

**Sternicza 11 · 14 November 2026 · schema 18 / v1.16.0**

The new default is three **9 m Thuja plicata ‘Excelsa’**, with a displayed plant subtotal of **28,500 PLN**. This substantially reduces the plant budget compared with the original **179,700 PLN** pine reference. It also reduces screening: from the seated living-room eye, the model hides **41.4% of PCK3 facade samples with Excelsa**, compared with **90.4% with the original broad pines**. From the terrace the corresponding results are **44.2% and 95.9%**. The cheaper default is therefore a deliberate cost/coverage comparison, not equivalent replacement screening.

All nine cases below use the same pool, hedges, ground levels, buildings and seven fixed eyes. Only the specimen trees change. No tree is moved into the central garden, and no future growth is credited. Figures are model sample fractions, not measured facade area or guarantees about living foliage.

### Crown assumptions that affect the result

The model uses opaque **cones** for thujas, spruces and firs, and opaque **ellipsoids** for pines. These are comparison envelopes rather than measured branch geometry. In particular, a cone's maximum width is at its base and falls to zero at the leader. A supplier's maximum overall width does not certify that exact profile.

| Option | Model crown base above planting ground | Width basis | Depth and profile |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Excelsa | 0.30 m | Published 2 m | Depth 2 m and conical profile assumed |
| Original pines | 2.50 m | 6 / 6 / 5 m within the published 4–6 m band; 10 m height uses its upper endpoint | Matching circular depths and ellipsoidal profile assumed |
| White fir | 0.40 m | Published 4.5 m | Depth 4.5 m and conical profile assumed |
| Norway spruce | 0.40 m | **3 m assumed**, not supplied by the listing | Depth 3 m and conical profile assumed |
| Brabant | 0.30 m | **1.5 m assumed**, not supplied by the listing | Depth 1.5 m and conical profile assumed |
| Serbian spruce | 0.40 m | **Published 3 m** | Depth 3 m and conical profile assumed |
| De Bruyn pine | 2.00 m | **3 m assumed**, not supplied by the listing | Depth 3 m and ellipsoidal profile assumed |
| Nordmann fir | 0.40 m | Published 7 m | Depth 7 m and conical profile assumed |

All crown-base heights are assumptions. Current stock, foliage at the critical upper sightlines, asymmetry and branch gaps need actual specimen photographs and measurements. The catalogue price is not a quote for an enlarged assumed crown: the app requests a custom price when its supported size reference is exceeded.

## 5. What the comparison changes in the decision

**Excelsa is a budget-led starting point with a narrower physical footprint.** Relative to hedges alone, it adds about **7.5 percentage points** of wall screening from the seated house eye, **11.0 points** from the terrace, **3.8 points** from the jacuzzi and **20.7 points** from the garden. Most tested house/terrace/jacuzzi roof points remain visible. Three narrow crowns at these widely spaced reference centres do not form a continuous tall hedge.

**Brabant is the lowest plant-price option, with a small immediate improvement from the house.** At the assumed 6.5 × 1.5 m size, all 365 roof samples remain visible from both house eyes, terrace and jacuzzi. Its 8,400 PLN subtotal is net. No rapid-growth uplift is used to disguise the shorter supplied height.

**The 7 m Serbian spruce is a useful counterexample to choosing on height alone.** Its wider 3 m model crown slightly improves garden and fire-lounge results relative to the taller 2 m-wide Excelsa, while Excelsa performs better from the house, terrace and jacuzzi. Both remain partial screens.

**Norway spruce offers more modeled coverage than Excelsa for 39,603 PLN in plants**, but the 3 m width is unverified. The study establishes the consequence of that assumed crown, not the coverage of any available De Bruyn specimen. Obtain the width and actual foliage profile before treating this as a purchasing comparison.

**White fir improves the garden and fire-lounge wall results more substantially.** Its published maximum width provides a firmer dimensional starting point than the unquoted Norway spruce width. The conical upper crown still leaves 333 of 365 roof points visible from the seated house eye at these positions, so the 9 m height and 4.5 m maximum spread do not establish a high continuous screen.

**The imported pine keeps more width high in its ellipsoidal model crown**, which explains why its 8.5 m × 3 m envelope outperforms several taller conical options from the house. That is a geometric inference. Its actual width, clear stem and branch density are unmeasured, so it cannot inherit the 6 / 6 / 5 m crown performance of the original pines at the lower quoted price.

**Nordmann fir gives the strongest screening among the seven new options in this fixed-position test**, including zero remaining sampled wall/roof points at the named garden and fire-lounge eyes. It still hides less from the main house, terrace and jacuzzi eyes than the broad pine reference. Its three 7 m crowns also create the most demanding space assumption: all extend outside the parcel and overhang modeled hardscape. The 84,000 PLN plant subtotal is not evidence that three such specimens can be installed here.

## 6. Space and procurement limits

Every case deliberately retains the requested trunk positions. Their minimum centre distances to the pool's full coping are approximately **1.00, 1.30 and 1.00 m**. A trunk passing the simulator's obstacle check does not establish root-ball or excavation clearance. No supplied root-ball dimensions are assumed in this study.

The Excelsa and Brabant nominal crown outlines remain within the mapped parcel. Excelsa tree 1 nevertheless triggers the model's hardscape-overhang warning, so even its smaller footprint is not a clearance approval. Norway spruce, Serbian spruce and the imported pine extend beyond the boundary at tree 1. All three original pine, white-fir and Nordmann crowns extend beyond the parcel. The ray calculation credits the **full** crown envelopes, including those portions outside the boundary.

For any shortlisted option, the decision needs tagged individual specimens, measured upper-crown profiles, root-ball dimensions, final VAT-inclusive plant prices and a complete delivery/planting proposal. These matters can change the ranking. This report compares the requested positions; it does not optimise a new row, increase tree counts, predict growth, or assess future shade and maintenance.

## 7. Reproduce the results

From `garden-lab`, run:

```sh
node scripts/compare-tree-alternatives.mjs /tmp/tree-alternatives-2026-11-14.json
```

Without a filename, the script prints JSON to standard output. The output includes the frozen settings, full-precision eyes, tree geometry, source price/tax notes, all exact wall and roof counts, additional samples hidden over the hedge-only control, and placement warnings. The script verifies unchanged sample denominators between cases and keeps installation unquoted. It fails if a dated price or crown form has changed, so a later catalogue revision cannot silently rewrite this comparison.
