# Building 4h: making the blank boundary wall part of the garden

Research and price check: **14 November 2026**. This note addresses the neighbour’s blank garden-facing wall, separately from the taller, more distant PCK3 building. It is a concept and procurement brief, not a measured façade survey or a contractor quotation.

## Recommendation

The best first proposal is **an agreed repair and muted repaint of 4h’s garden-facing elevation, followed by carefully selected evergreen climber panels**, while retaining the four-sided perimeter hedge. If the owner will not allow work on the wall, use a structure supported entirely on the Sternicza 11 side and screen the most important seating views locally. A ten-metre-high independent green structure is a substantial construction project; it should not be presented as a cheap trellis.

This wall has no windows facing the garden, according to the user’s direct observation. Its problem is visual bulk and appearance, rather than overlooking through this elevation. A calm, deliberately finished wall can therefore be a much better outcome than attempting to hide every square metre behind additional large trees. It also preserves the open centre of the garden.

For the desired quiet, organic spa garden, my design preference is a matte, muted warm grey, pale taupe or restrained grey-green finish, tested on large samples from the living room and spa seats. A deliberately limited climbing pattern can soften the plane without making a patchy first-year installation look unfinished. These colour and composition choices are design judgments, not guarantees that the wall will disappear. Bright white, a very dark solid plane, a full-wall mirror and a strongly illuminated wall would each risk making the surface more noticeable.

### 1. Repaint: a relatively direct way to improve every view

The wall should first be identified as bare masonry, mineral render, polymer render, painted render or an insulated façade system. Then choose a compatible repair, primer and coating specification. “Mineral paint” is not a universal instruction to apply ordinary silicate paint to any existing surface. KEIM distinguishes traditional mineral-substrate products from its sol-silicate Soldalit system, which is described as suitable for mineral, organic and mixed substrates, including external insulation systems. Product selection still depends on the existing coating’s condition. [KEIM Soldalit](https://www.keim.com/pl-pl/produkty/farby-elewacyjne/soldalit/)

Choose the colour with that façade specification. Dark surfaces impose higher thermal demands on insulated render; Caparol’s guidance makes allowable dark colours dependent on the system, visible reflectance and total solar reflectance, with manufacturer-confirmed values. This is another reason to start with muted warm greige or restrained olive rather than near-black. The product-specific thresholds are not a universal permission to repaint an unknown existing façade. [Caparol, dark colours on ETICS](https://www.caparol.pl/aktualnosci/ciemne-kolory-na-systemach-etics)

Propose three physical sample areas and evaluate them from indoors, the jacuzzi and the rear garden in sun and overcast light. A repaint can improve the experience immediately; its extent and colour must be agreed with the wall’s owner. Repair open cracks or failing render before using planting to conceal them.

### 2. Evergreen climbing greenery: the strongest plant-based wall treatment

The first cultivar to investigate is **Hedera helix ‘Białystok’**, rather than selecting an unspecified ivy simply because a catalogue calls it evergreen. Polish specialist grower Clematis describes it as a cultivar selected in north-eastern Poland, suitable for shade or partial shade, with evergreen leaves, approximately one metre of annual growth and an eventual height around twenty metres. The nursery lists hardiness zones 5B–10. These are grower descriptions, not a guarantee for an individual exposed façade. [Clematis cultivar record](https://www.clematis.com.pl/en/encyklopedia/?tx_plant_pi1%5Bplant%5D=998)

**The timing is decisive.** Even a suitable vigorous ivy is not a one-season solution for a ten-metre wall from ordinary nursery plants. RHS gives generic common ivy a ten-to-twenty-year maturity range, while its wall guidance describes growth to a two-storey house over roughly fifteen to twenty years. Purchasing larger, trained plants improves the starting point, but does not turn a small root system into an instant full-height canopy. [RHS species record](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/43091/hedera-helix/details) · [RHS wall guidance](https://www.rhs.org.uk/prevention-protection/ivy-on-buildings)

There are two materially different installation choices:

- **Direct attachment to the neighbour’s wall:** the smallest support cost, but only after the owner agrees and the façade is inspected. Ivy’s attachment roots are not the same as roots penetrating sound brickwork. Historic England’s research finds that ivy can buffer temperature and wetting cycles on sound masonry, while existing cracks, loose mortar, roof details and gutters can create damage or access problems. Removal itself can harm weak surfaces. This evidence concerns masonry and historic walls; it is not blanket approval for unknown insulated render. [Historic England, Ivy on Walls](https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/technical-advice/monuments-and-sites/ivy-on-walls/)
- **A separate supporting mesh or cable plane:** makes the intended plant extent easier to control and can keep vegetation away from the wall. If its posts and foundations are entirely independent, it avoids relying on the neighbour’s façade for structural support. If cables or brackets attach to that façade, it remains an owner-approved façade installation. The standoff, anchor arrangement, wet vegetation load, wind load and maintenance route must be designed together; “leave an air gap” alone is not a structural specification. Jakob supplies engineered cable/mesh systems and dedicated brackets for insulated façades. [Jakob systems](https://www.jakob.com/pl/en/applications/greening) · [Insulated-façade brackets](https://www.jakob.com/pl/en/portfolio/greensolutions)

Plan the rooting zone alongside the existing thujas rather than assuming both can occupy the same narrow bed without consequences. A dense four-metre hedge may shade the base, compete for water and obscure access to supports. The detailed scheme needs a coherent planting and access section, including where installers can stand and how the upper planting will be pruned. Jakob recommends planning maintenance at the outset, with inspections and horticultural work at intervals depending on the planting, commonly six to twelve months. [Jakob planning guide](https://www.jakob.com/be/en/news/first-planning-steps-for-greening)

### 3. Deciduous climbers: beautiful, but seasonal

**Climbing hydrangea, Hydrangea anomala subsp. petiolaris**, is an attractive candidate for sheltered, partially shaded conditions with moist, well-drained soil. RHS lists it as deciduous, eventually exceeding twelve metres and spreading four to eight metres. Its flowers and branch structure can be valuable even where complete winter screening is not required. [RHS climbing hydrangea](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/97624/hydrangea-anomala-subsp-petiolaris/details)

**Virginia creeper, Parthenocissus quinquefolia**, is also deciduous. RHS gives an eventual height above twelve metres, four-to-eight-metre spread and five-to-ten-year maturity. It can produce strong seasonal coverage and autumn colour, but the wall returns to view after leaf fall. That is a poor match for a promise of year-round concealment. Its UK invasive-species classification should not be misrepresented as a Polish prohibition; local planting suitability needs separate consideration. [RHS Virginia creeper](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/12349/parthenocissus-quinquefolia/details)

### 4. Modular living wall: reserve for a smaller special feature

Polish supplier Garden Plus lists an outdoor Verti Green system with approximately **85 kg/m²** loading, integrated drainage and irrigation, and a published starting price of **2,800 PLN/m²**. The supplier offers project-specific structure and plant selection, including winter irrigation arrangements. VAT and the full scope of that headline price are not explicit on the retrieved page. [Garden Plus Verti Green](https://www.zielonesciany.pl/vertigreen-new)

Applied to the model’s gross 134.31 m² face, these figures imply approximately **376,000 PLN** at the starting rate and **11.4 tonnes** at the stated area loading. Both are illustrative multiplication, not a quote or an engineering load case. Even an 80 m² feature would start around **224,000 PLN** before clarifying project inclusions. This makes a smaller feature panel more plausible than treating the entire neighbour’s wall as a vertical garden.

An independent living wall also needs foundations and support; a wall-mounted one needs owner agreement and a verified load path. The design must state irrigation, winter operation, access, plant replacement, drainage and maintenance responsibilities. These services are part of the landscape’s ongoing operation, not incidental extras.

## Earlier brainstorms: projector, mirror and climbing wall

### Projector

A projector could make an enjoyable occasional **evening** feature on an agreed surface. It cannot reliably disguise the building in normal daylight. Projected light adds to ambient illumination; it cannot paint a darker landscape over a sunlit wall. As a scale illustration, 5,000 lumens spread uniformly across 134 m² is only about 37 lux before real-world losses. BenQ’s outdoor guidance explicitly identifies ambient light and direct sun as limitations, with darker conditions and smaller images improving performance. Its small portable-projector advice should not be extrapolated to a ten-metre façade. [BenQ outdoor projection guidance](https://www.benq.eu/en-uk/knowledge-center/knowledge/projectors-for-daytime-outdoors.html)

If desired, design a smaller cinema area, weather protection, cable route and neighbour-agreed operating hours. Keep this separate from the daytime screening strategy.

### Giant mirror

I would not make a full-wall mirror the main solution. It would reflect a different scene from every viewpoint, potentially including PCK3, bright sky or neighbouring structures, rather than consistently showing attractive planting. Large outdoor reflective panels also become a support, fixing, cleaning and glare-control project.

The bird risk is relevant to an installation intended to reflect trees and sky: USGS describes those reflections as a reason birds mistake glass for usable habitat, and notes territorial attacks on reflections. A small decorative mirror can be evaluated with external visibility markings and careful placement, but an unbroken mirrored wall is a poor fit for a planted garden. [USGS glass/reflection guidance](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-can-i-stop-birds-repeatedly-hitting-my-windows)

### Climbing wall

Given the owner’s interest in climbing, a limited climbing feature could create genuine use. A ten-metre neighbour-mounted route, however, is a sport structure, not decorative façade cladding. It needs a verified structural design, an appropriate landing or rope/belay arrangement, safe separation from pool furniture and paths, and agreement on ownership, access and maintenance.

Outdoor wall manufacturer Walltopia specifically discusses additional wind, snow, water and temperature demands and uses outdoor-specific protective materials. EP Climbing describes routine, operational and annual expert inspections under its EN 12572 maintenance approach. These sources establish the engineering and operational scope; they do not establish Polish planning permission for this particular wall. [Walltopia outdoor walls](https://walltopia.com/products/outdoor-climbing-walls/) · [EP inspection guidance](https://epclimbing.com/en/services/inspection-and-maintenance)

My preference would be a smaller independently supported training feature in a designated activity area if climbing is itself a priority. It should not consume the principal spa view simply to cover an ugly building.

## Ownership, permissions and the actual boundary

The Polish Civil Code protects the owner’s use of their property (Article 140), addresses unreasonable interference with neighbours (144), and allows specified action on encroaching roots and branches (150). Article 154’s presumption about shared use of boundary devices is not evidence that this building façade belongs to the garden owner. Do not assume that a wall can be painted or drilled simply because it faces the plot. [2026 consolidated Civil Code](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2026/795/text/T/D20260795L.pdf)

For an agreed façade scheme, record the design, permitted attachment method, maintenance access, pruning limits, liability for damage, drainage, costs and removal/reinstatement arrangements. Establish who owns the façade and who can authorise work if the building has multiple owners. An independent structure still needs its actual boundary and foundation positions checked.

The 2026 Building Law lists **fences above 2.20 m** among works requiring notification rather than a building permit; fences not exceeding that height are listed separately as exempt from both. That does not classify a ten-metre planted structure, nor does it create a general 2.20 m limit for living hedges. The design’s actual construction and local context determine its route. [Building Law, Article 29](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2026/524/text/I/D20260524.pdf)

The parcel-specific local plan and any conservation constraints were **not established** in this research. The municipal spatial-planning portal is the starting point for checking parcels `226101_1.0034.400` and `226101_1.0034.401`, plus the neighbouring building parcel. The Gdańsk landscape resolution includes advertising rules; those must not be applied indiscriminately to ordinary non-commercial garden art. [Gdańsk spatial-planning portal](https://www.gdansk.pl/zagospodarowanie-przestrzenne-gdanska)

## Recommended sequence and procurement brief

1. **Measure and identify.** Confirm façade ownership, wall construction, roof/service details, exact boundary and the actual extent visible from the living room, terrace and spa. Keep the confirmed windowless elevation in every simulator scenario.
2. **Prepare a concrete repaint proposal.** Present three colour samples and a defined elevation drawing, with an offer to fund agreed repair and painting. Ask contractors for explicit preparation, coating, access, VAT and warranty inclusions.
3. **Design the long-term planting as a complete section.** Compare direct ‘Białystok’ ivy only on an approved suitable substrate against a separated mesh system. Resolve thujas, roots, soil, watering and maintenance access together. Show installation-day and developed foliage separately.
4. **Test the important views before adding height.** A locally positioned canopy may conceal the upper wall from the jacuzzi or dining area more efficiently than a continuous high green structure. Keep the centre lawn open.
5. **Reserve premium features for a deliberate use.** A limited living-wall panel, evening projection surface or small training wall can be worthwhile if it is wanted as a feature in its own right. None should be presented as the default fix for every view.

For a contractor request, attach measured façade photographs, the boundary section, the four key viewing positions, preferred immediate appearance, expected mature planting extent, the maximum garden strip available, and the requirement to quote installation and annual maintenance separately. This turns the next decision into a comparison of deliverable schemes rather than another change of tree positions.
