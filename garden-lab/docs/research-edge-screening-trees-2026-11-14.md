# PCK3: mature trees and screening along the plot edges

Research checked: **14 November 2026**. Location: Sternicza 11, Gdańsk. Objective: substantial screening immediately or within one year, with the central lawn kept open. The working design already includes a 4 m perimeter hedge, three approximately 10 m Austrian pines and a proposed 0.5 m garden raise.

This is a botanical, nursery-stock and procurement assessment. The separate geometric study determines which parts of PCK3 remain visible from each viewpoint. Published product listings below are price references or leads for quotations, **not received supplier quotations, reserved stock or confirmed deliveries to Gdańsk**. No suppliers have been contacted for this report.

## 1. Recommendation

**Keep the requested pine arrangement as the design baseline and investigate a taller evergreen strip only along the edge sectors through which PCK3 remains visible.** The strongest new procurement lead is **Thuja plicata ‘Excelsa’ in a 12–13 m catalogue size**. Unlike a conventional retail hedge, it approaches the height needed for an edge-only solution, but its actual foliage profile and opacity remain unverified. Smaller dense Thuja plicata and Thuja occidentalis ‘Brabant’ are supplemental options. Serbian spruce is useful where exposure and soil suit it. None should be substituted for the pines simply by keeping the same three trunk positions and assuming equal screening. [S46]

The reason is physical. The existing 4 m hedge already handles much of the low-level view. What remains is foliage in the correct horizontal positions **above that hedge**, including sufficient width near the top of the screen. A tree that is 10 m tall only at its leader can leave much of a building visible on either side of that point. A conical spruce or thuja may therefore require more stems than a broad-crowned pine to cover the same upper-storey view. This is a geometric inference from the different crown forms, not a measured performance claim for an unpurchased tree.

For an immediate result, I would investigate these alternatives:

1. **Specialist tall Excelsa specimens forming an edge screen**, subject to measurement of the actual trees. This is the first alternative to investigate for height. The cultivar itself can be loosely branched, so a promise of a dense hedge cannot be inferred from its name or overall height. [S49, S50]
2. **Selected 10–11 m Austrian pines with measured broad crowns, arranged along the permitted corner and boundary sectors.** This preserves the informal, sculptural garden character. Genuine catalogue leads exist, including crowns described as 6–7 m wide. Their large root balls and lifting weights are consequential. [S1–S4]
3. **A short section of 6–9 m dense thuja behind or beside the grove, occupying the missing sightline sector.** It can provide a continuous middle-height green backdrop more economically than additional giant specimen pines. It remains a partial solution wherever the model requires an opaque screen above the available plant height. [S5–S8]
4. **A hybrid of edge planting and a carefully positioned pergola, canopy or side screen near an occupied area.** The nearby structure can intercept high sightlines from the terrace or jacuzzi without planting a tree in the middle of the lawn. It must be checked from each occupied position; one sheltered seating zone does not imply complete concealment from every place in the garden.

I would **not** spend the budget on bamboo, standard pleached hornbeam or Mediterranean evergreen trees as the primary answer to PCK3's upper floors. Their available height, winter behaviour or cold-exposure requirements do not match this particular immediate-screening requirement. They remain useful ingredients for atmosphere and lower-level enclosure. [S17–S24]

## 2. What a planting design must actually buy

The height of the visible building alone does not specify the height of the needed screen. For a straight sightline on a common height datum:

`required screen-top elevation = eye elevation + (distance to screen / distance to target) × (target elevation − eye elevation)`

The tree's required height above its planting ground is that elevation minus the planting-ground elevation. The proposed garden raise must be counted once. It cannot be added separately to both the tree height and its base elevation.

The same exercise is needed horizontally. From different viewpoints, the line to the left or right edge of PCK3 may cross different plot-edge sectors. A dense corner cluster can look convincing in one image while leaving another viewpoint exposed. The plant species cannot change this geometry.

For each candidate tree, obtain **height and foliage width at several elevations**, particularly around the height of the exposed windows and roofline in the projected view. The maximum canopy diameter is insufficient: it may occur much lower down. Record the height of the first substantial foliage, uneven or missing sectors, and the intended orientation of the tree. A supplied tree should be represented by those measurements rather than a perfectly opaque ellipsoid.

The research verified catalogue leads up to approximately 11 m for Austrian pine, 9 m for Brabant and 13 m for Excelsa. It did **not** verify a tagged, fully furnished giant screen with a confirmed quantity and delivered-and-installed price. No 13–14 m Thuja listing was verified. That is an unresolved procurement task, not proof that specialist sourcing is impossible.

### A narrow crown is not automatically a space-saving solution

Serbian spruce's narrow habit saves space per tree, but it also provides less horizontal coverage per stem near the roofline. Where its top is pointed, another tall tree may be needed beside it. Enough trees to make a continuous high screen can consume a surprisingly long strip and still need meaningful root space. Keeping lower branches alive also needs light; overcrowding is not a free method of increasing opacity. The recommendation is therefore to compare **the whole planted section**, not one pine against one spruce. [S12, S13, S26]

### Can the hedge be kept very thin?

Regular shaping can control width, but most conifers cannot reliably recover after cutting into old brown wood. RHS identifies yew as a reliable exception and Thuja as less reliable. A narrow hedge needs an agreed maintained profile from the outset, access for trimming and light reaching its lower sides. An 8–10 m hedge is a substantial maintenance asset, even when its plan-view footprint looks small. [S16, S31]

### Is giant Excelsa the edge-only answer?

It is the most relevant specialist lead found, but the missing measurements are decisive. The maximum listed crown width is not a basal width and does not describe how much foliage remains near the leader. The supplier does not publish clear-stem height or width at the critical upper elevations. A line of pointed crowns can leave triangular sky gaps through which PCK3 remains visible. The Polish botanical description's sparse basal branching also means this must be evaluated together with the existing lower hedge, not assumed to replace it. [S46, S49]

The nursery's website uses a stock-availability heading, but this does not confirm sufficient matching trees for the project, current reservations, or transport to Gdańsk. Ask for tagged photographs and dimensions before using a solid green wall in the simulator. A separately priced Excelsa product is explicitly described as a **shaped high-stem tree**, demonstrating why form must be checked independently of the cultivar. [S46, S48]

### Price traps found during the research

- **The former De Bruyn €5,878 lead is no longer the displayed price.** Both the English and Dutch catalogues now show €6,174 for the ordinary 8–9 m wired-ball class. The published 10% private-customer discount starts at ten plants of the same species and size; it does not automatically apply to three trees. [S1, S38]
- **NewGarden's 7–8 m Atrovirens reference is inconsistent.** A FAQ still mentions €1,787.90 and links an older product, but the link redirects to a general page whose selectable current variants stop at 5–6 m, currently €799.90 including VAT plus freight. The 7–8 m entry is excluded from the verified current-price table pending supplier confirmation. [S8]
- **Circumference is not height.** Nursery entries such as 30/35, 60/70 or 100/120 often denote trunk circumference in centimetres. Similarly, a cultivar's eventual 15–20 m height is not the supplied size. Always match the dimensions to the exact selected variant.

## 5. Installation constraints that change the layout

### The root ball sets a real minimum envelope

The pine example's 2.6 m ball extends approximately **1.3 m from its centre** before preparation space. A trunk drawn 1 m from pool coping cannot automatically accommodate it. The pool shell, services and trunk position within the ball must be checked. Its mass requires an appropriate lifting plan; these dimensions do not establish a universal safe tree-to-pool distance. [S4] The giant Excelsa's 2.7 m ball has a corresponding radius of 1.35 m. It is therefore not a way to fit giant roots into the existing 1 m clearances. [S46]

Van den Berk recommends a planting-hole diameter around 1.5–2 times the root-ball diameter and warns against deeper planting, particularly where heavy balls can settle in new construction ground. Its advice is to anchor new trees and provide watering that reaches the whole ball. The broad preparation zone may be coordinated with landscape construction, but cannot simply be assumed to fit between an already-built pool and a boundary. [S39]

### The garden raise must support roots, not bury trunks

Finish the proposed raised grade and root-zone design as one operation. Root flare should be at the correct final surface; adding 0.5 m of soil around an already-planted stem is not equivalent to planting the tree 0.5 m higher. RHS explains that deep planting restricts air movement and can cause establishment failure. Compaction and poor drainage are also significant establishment problems. [S40, S41]

### Immediate visual effect needs several seasons of aftercare

Buying mature stock buys existing foliage, not instant establishment. Budget monitoring, irrigation, anchoring adjustment and a replacement obligation into the installation contract. RHS recommends watering large newly planted specimens through as many as three summers; Van den Berk recommends additional water for at least two years, adjusted to weather and root-ball moisture. Overwatering is also harmful. [S42, S43]

The nursery's lifting window is relevant: Baumschule 2000 describes root-balled planting from approximately mid-September to the end of April, and Galeria generally describes large field-grown deliveries from the latter half of September to mid-May, depending on weather. Frozen or waterlogged ground may still prevent planting within those calendar windows. [S5, S44, S40]

### Exposure must be checked at canopy height

The existing hedge may shelter low foliage while a 10 m tree rises well above it. Salt tolerance, winter hardiness, wind tolerance and rooting stability are separate characteristics. In particular, Serbian spruce's narrow outline should not be taken as proof of wind suitability. RHS advises establishing a wind-filtering shelter belt on exposed coastal sites and notes that very solid barriers can produce turbulence. That is relevant to both the tall hedge and any proposed structural screen. [S12–S14, S45]

## 6. The blank 4h wall: botanical role

The blank wall needs a separate visual treatment from PCK3. Since the user confirms it has no garden-facing windows, the aim is to replace the view of masonry with an attractive foreground, while the other site's height and planning constraints are checked independently.

The strongest plant options are **a controlled yew backdrop**, a suitable evergreen hedge or a trained climber on an independently supported structure. Pleached hornbeam can soften an exposed wall in summer but will not guarantee full winter opacity. A dark green hedge, varied lower planting and carefully placed lighting can make a much better garden composition than trying to obscure every high surface with an oversized tree. The actual wall dimensions and shade determine whether the available 4–7 m stock is useful; no complete wall-concealment claim is made here. [S15, S16, S19–S21]

A decorative finish on the neighbour's wall, a climber attached to it or a roof-level intervention requires the separate owner/structural assessment covered in the companion wall study. This report does not assume access to, or permission to alter, that building.

## 7. Specification for comparable quotations

The next useful supplier request should attach the permitted planting sectors and the required foliage envelope from the sightline model. It should request **tagged individual specimens**, so that selection is based on the actual plants.

| Required information | Why it changes the decision |
|---|---|
| Exact botanical name, form, nursery location, individual tree ID and current photographs from several sides | Prevents comparison of different forms or illustrative catalogue images. |
| Height above root flare; minimum accepted height after transport and any pruning | A 9–10 m size band is insufficient if the design needs a true 10 m minimum. |
| Crown width and depth at 4, 6, 8 and 9 m, plus near the tree top; first substantial foliage height | Determines whether the actual branches interrupt the target windows and roofline. |
| Root-ball diameter, depth, mass, trunk position within the ball and transplanting history | Determines whether the specimen fits, how it can be lifted, and what access is required. |
| Confirmed quantity and reservation period; earliest possible lifting, delivery and planting date | Separates a catalogue class from available stock. |
| Plant-only net and gross price; currency, VAT basis and any quantity discount | Makes quotations financially comparable. |
| Packing, transport to Gdańsk, abnormal-load arrangements if needed, unloading and final positioning | Captures the major costs absent from an online plant price. |
| Site preparation, drainage, anchoring, irrigation, planting and making good | Turns the tree purchase into a complete usable installation. |
| Two to three growing seasons of aftercare, inspection dates and replacement terms | Protects the intended screening outcome during establishment. |
| Agreed substitutions and dimensional tolerances | Prevents a smaller, differently shaped tree being supplied under a broad catalogue description. |

Galeria's terms explicitly say that ordinary photographs are illustrative and describe possible dimensional variation of 15–20%. The project should therefore agree the measured screening dimensions in writing for the chosen trees. This is a procurement recommendation; it is not an interpretation of the legal enforceability of individual contract clauses. [S32]

## 8. Decision to make after modelling

First model the specialist Excelsa option using conservative conical crowns, realistic planting offsets and the available height band. If it is promising, obtain actual specimen measurements and test those before choosing it. Also test whether additional broad edge crowns can cover the important viewpoints with a less formal appearance. If complete coverage requires a continuous edge screen materially taller or broader near its top than obtainable stock, the alternatives are to accept a residual roofline, reserve a larger boundary planting strip for a specialist mature screen, or use a nearby canopy/screen for the most important occupied viewpoints.

For this project, **species selection should follow the required foliage envelope**. The most promising new investigation is the giant Excelsa lead, potentially combined with the requested pine grove and lower hedge. Replacing the grove with three narrow trees, waiting for nominally fast-growing plants, or buying a standard pleached screen would not establish the desired whole-building result.
